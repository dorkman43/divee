02 — One shortcut

One ⌥Space and you're there.

Hover a row or press → and it slides out to the side, just like a Windows right-click menu — up to three levels deep.

  • Fully keyboard-driven. ↑↓ move · → open · ← back · Return run · ESC close.
  • Turn on only what you need. Modules you turn off don't run at all.
  • A little mascot lives in your menu bar — Divee by default, or the cat if you prefer.

04 — Fan & Temp

Fans and temperatures, under control.

CPU and GPU temperatures and a slider per fan. Drag and release to put that fan in manual mode, or choose Auto · Max · Temperature curve.

  • Reading temperatures and RPM needs no permission.
  • Changing fan speed uses a small background helper you approve once in Login Items.
  • If Divee stops responding, the helper returns the fans to automatic within 30 seconds.
05 — Sleep

Keep awake. Even with the lid closed.

✓ Keep Awake and ✓ Clamshell (stays on with the lid closed), for as long as you need: indefinitely · 30 min · 1 · 2 · 4 hours.

  • Or just say it: “Keep my Mac awake for 30 minutes”.
  • Clamshell shows a heat warning when turned on, and turns itself off when Divee quits or stops responding.
06 — Lightweight

Light enough to forget it's there.

About 0.2% CPU and ~42 MB of memory while the panel is closed, about 1% CPU while it's open. The app is 13 MB.

  • CPU with recent history, memory, network, uptime and free disk space — right in the panel.
  • Animations are native macOS ones, and you can turn them off in Settings.
  • Out of the box only System, Battery, Fan & Temp and Checklist are on.

08 — Privacy

On your Mac by default.

Data leaves your Mac only when you turn on a feature that needs it, and Settings › About lists exactly what's on.

  • Nothing collected.No analytics, telemetry or ad tracking. Keystrokes are never recorded; the screen and camera are looked at once, only when you ask, and never saved.
  • Key rules stay local.Keyboard rules run in a separate, sandboxed process that can't reach the network. It tells Divee only what to do, never which keys you pressed.
  • Risky actions ask first.When the AI wants to do something that can't easily be undone, the mascot shows what it's about to do and waits: ↩ to run, ESC to cancel.
  • Notarized and signed.Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple. Every update and the update feed are signed (EdDSA), and nothing installs until you say so.

09 — FAQ

Questions

Is Divee free?

Yes. Divee is free to use.

What do I need to run it?

macOS 14 Sonoma or later on an Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or later). A few features need newer macOS: Apple's on-device model needs macOS 26, and on-device translation needs macOS 15.

Does the AI send my data to the cloud?

Not by default. The AI is local-first: Apple's on-device model, then Ollama, both on your Mac. It hands off to a server only if you've chosen one, and only when it's needed or you say “use the cloud” — those answers are marked ☁︎.

Why does it ask for permissions?

Only the features you turn on ask, and they say why — for example, Accessibility for key remap cards and Window Snap, or Screen Recording for Capture. Fan control and Clamshell use a background helper you approve once in System Settings › General › Login Items.

How do updates work?

Divee checks once a day (you can turn it off in Settings › About). When a new version is out, a small dot appears on the menu bar mascot — nothing installs until you click Install.

How do I uninstall it?

If you used fan control or Clamshell, click Remove Helper in Settings › Fan & Temp first — it restores your fan and sleep settings. Then quit Divee and move it to the Trash.
